I've recently been devouring biographies.

Nothing unusual about that. A stack of them - Pamela Churchill Harriman, Joe Carstairs, McNamara's memoir about Vietnam, a deep dive into the Kennedy family in the war years.

I wasn't looking for anything in particular.

Then I started noticing something strange.

The same women kept appearing. Across completely unrelated books. Different decades, different continents, different wars. And they were always in the same position: right next to the moment that changed everything. Standing behind the man at the podium. Mentioned in a footnote. Named once, never again.

They were doing the actual work. And history had simply… looked past them.

One night I was lying in bed and it hit me hard enough that I said it out loud to the ceiling:

Fuck.

There's a force in the world - in the lives of women across centuries - that we've been mislabeling our entire lives. We look at our grandmothers and we call it stoicism. Endurance. Quiet strength.

That's the wrong word.

They weren't enduring. They were driven. By the same force you feel. The same thing that makes you get up and do what needs doing even when no one is watching and no one will remember.

These women moved history. History just didn't bother to mention them.

Lost Letters is my attempt to fix that.

- Rachel Lilley

Season 1: The Lady Bird Special

October 1964. Eight states. 47 stops. 1,682 miles. Alone.

Here is what you probably know about Lady Bird Johnson:

She was a First Lady. She was dignified. She liked wildflowers.

Here is what the textbook left out:

In the autumn of 1964, Lyndon Johnson had just signed the Civil Rights Act. The South was turning against him - fast and hard. His campaign needed a rescue operation in states that were about to abandon the Democratic Party entirely.

His advisor walked into Lady Bird's sitting room with a map of eight Southern states and said:

"You are the only person in this administration who can do this."

What she was being asked to do: board a 19-car train, travel 1,682 miles through the Deep South, and stand on the back platform at 47 stops - alone - in front of crowds that had already decided to hate her husband.

Crowds that held signs reading BLACK BIRD GO HOME.

Crowds where the FBI had received specific bomb threats.

Crowds in states where her husband's signature had just outlawed the world those people had built their lives around.

She was afraid. She wrote about it in her diary.

She got on the train anyway.

Season 1 of Lost Letters rides that train with her.

Twelve letters. Twelve moments from those four days in October. Her voice, her words, her diary entries, her fear, her fury, her grace under the kind of pressure most of us will never understand.

Primary sources from the LBJ Library, Liz Carpenter's memoir, FBI records, and Lady Bird's own audio diary. Nothing invented. Everything real.

What Lost Letters is

Lost Letters is a subscription that puts a real letter in your hands.

Not a newsletter. Not a Substack. A letter - designed, printed, and mailed to your door. With a handwritten signature at the bottom. From the desk of Rachel Lilley.

Each season covers one woman. One event. Twelve letters.

Season 1 is twelve letters across the four days of the Lady Bird Special - one letter per major stop on the tour. Each one drops you inside a specific moment: the pitch, the departure, the first hostile crowd, the night she almost turned back, the moment she didn't.

You'll read things no biography has ever put in front of you in this format - because biographies don't take you inside a single hour the way a letter can.
